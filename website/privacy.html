Privacy policy

Effective 25 August 2026 · Last updated 25 August 2026

This policy explains how the vBank mobile application (“vBank”, “the app”) handles information. It is written to be read by the people who actually use it — members of savings groups — and to satisfy the disclosure requirements of the Google Play Store and the Apple App Store.

The short version. vBank has no servers and no accounts. We do not collect, receive, transmit to ourselves, sell or share any of your personal data. Your records live on your own phone, encrypted, and are exchanged directly with the phones of the group members you invite — also encrypted. There is no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party SDK that phones home.

1. Who is responsible

vBank is published by the vBank project (“we”, “us”). Because the app has no backend, we do not act as a processor or controller of your group's records — we never receive them. For questions about this policy, write to privacy@vbank.zm.

3. Information we collect: none

We operate no servers for vBank. The app makes no requests to us, contains no analytics or crash-reporting SDK, and shows no advertising. We cannot see your groups, your balances, your identity or even how many people use the app.

4. What is shared with other people, and how

When you record something in a group, that record is encrypted and published to the peer-to-peer IPFS network so the other members of that group can receive it. This means:

  • Who can read it: only people holding your group's passphrase — in practice, the members your admins invited. Encryption is XChaCha20-Poly1305 with a key derived from that passphrase (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, 100,000 iterations, salted per group).
  • What other members see: your display name, your public key, the records you signed, and the amounts and dates in them. This is the same information a paper ledger would show the group.
  • What third parties see: other computers participating in the IPFS network may relay, store or cache the encrypted blocks and can observe network-level metadata (that a peer with a given peer ID is publishing or requesting content identifiers, and its IP address). They cannot read the contents.
  • What we see: nothing.
Public network, private contents. IPFS is a public, distributed network. Encrypted blocks that other nodes have cached cannot be recalled or deleted by us or by you. They remain unreadable without your group's passphrase, but you should treat publication as permanent — which is also why the passphrase must never be shared outside the group.

6. Security

  • The on-device database is encrypted with SQLCipher (AES-256). Its key is derived from a device secret stored in the platform's secure storage (Android Keystore / iOS Keychain), not in the app's files.
  • Every record is signed with Ed25519 and verified on receipt; records that fail verification, or that come from someone without the role to write them, are rejected.
  • Records in transit and at rest on the network are encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305 using your group's derived key. Backup files use the same construction with a key derived from your PIN.
  • Invites are one-use, expiring and signed by the admin who created them.

No system is perfect, and two risks are inherent to the design: anyone who learns your group passphrase can read that group's records, and anyone who obtains a backup file and its PIN can act as you. Guard both.

7. Children

vBank is intended for adults managing group finances and is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone — including children — because we collect nothing at all. Where a minor participates in a family or school savings group, a parent, guardian or group administrator should manage the app on their behaf and is responsible for the records entered.

8. Retention and deletion

  • Your device: records stay until you delete them. Settings → Log out removes your identity, keys and local database from the phone; uninstalling the app does the same. Neither can be undone without a backup file.
  • Other members' devices: records you sent are their copies of the group's books and remain with them, exactly as entries in a paper ledger would. Ask your group's admins to remove you from the roster to stop receiving further updates.
  • The network: encrypted blocks already cached by other IPFS nodes cannot be withdrawn (see section 4).
  • Us: we hold nothing, so there is nothing for us to delete and no data-deletion request to make. Details are on the deleting your data section of the home page.

9. International transfers

Because sync is peer-to-peer, your encrypted records travel directly to the devices of your group members and through IPFS nodes that may be located in other countries. The contents remain encrypted end to end with a key only your group holds. We do not transfer data ourselves because we do not receive any.

10. Your rights

Data-protection laws (including Zambia's Data Protection Act, and the GDPR where it applies) give you rights of access, correction, deletion, portability and objection. In vBank these are exercised directly in the app, because you hold the data:

  • Access and portability: everything about you is visible in the app; “Create backup” gives you a complete, portable copy.
  • Correction: ask an admin to record a reversal and the corrected entry.
  • Deletion: log out or uninstall (see section 8).
  • Complaints: if you believe the app mishandles data, write to privacy@vbank.zm; you may also complain to your national data-protection authority.

11. Third-party services

vBank includes no advertising, attribution or analytics SDKs. It uses open-source libraries that run entirely on your device (for cryptography, local storage, QR scanning, notifications and IPFS networking). The only external systems involved are the peer-to-peer network itself and, if you choose to use them, the apps you pick from the system share sheet when exporting a backup or an invite link — those apps' own privacy policies then apply.

12. Changes to this policy

If this policy changes we will update the date at the top and, where the change is significant, note it in the app's release notes. Continuing to use the app after a change means you accept the updated policy.
