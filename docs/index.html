What it is

A ledger for the way village banking actually works

Village banking (also called chilimba, merry-go-round, susu or ROSCA) is a group of people who meet regularly, each pay a fixed contribution into a common fund, and lend that fund to members at an agreed interest rate.

The books are usually kept in a paper ledger held by one person — which is fine until the book is lost, disputed, or the treasurer misses a meeting. vBank is that ledger, on the phones of every member at once. There is no company holding your money and no server holding your records: vBank moves no money. Cash is still handled in the room, exactly as it is today — vBank records what happened, proves who recorded it, and makes sure every member's copy agrees.

vBank is a record-keeping tool, not a bank. It is not a payment service, it holds no funds, and it is not regulated as a financial institution. Your group's money stays in your group's hands.
Who it is for

Treasurers keep the books. Members keep an eye on them.

For the treasurer and admins

Features

Everything a group needs, nothing it doesn't

Groups on your terms

Contribution amount and frequency, interest rate, late penalty, maximum loan multiple, minimum contributions before borrowing, and whether new members and loans need admin approval.

Contributions & penalties

Admins record contributions, penalties and withdrawals against a member. Every entry is signed and numbered, so two admins recording at once can never collide or overwrite each other.

The group fund split into what is available to lend and what is out on loan, with the interest still expected — so the group never lends money it doesn't have.

5

Schedule meetings, mark who was present, excused or absent, tick off who paid, and close the meeting with the total collected.

Group fund, contributions, loans out and repaid, penalties, and a per-member statement for any period — 1 month, 3 months, a year, or everything.

7

Corrections that leave a trail

Mistakes are reversed, not deleted: a member requests a reversal with a reason, an admin approves it, and both the original and the correction stay in the history.

Search and filter every long list — activity, transactions, members, loans and meetings — by name, amount, date, type or status.

Desktop, natively

Linux, macOS and Windows builds that follow each platform's own design system — Yaru on Ubuntu, macOS's sidebar and sheets, Fluent on Windows — over the same encrypted books.

How it works

Trust built into the records, not into a company

No server, no account

There is no sign-up, phone number or email. On first open vBank generates a cryptographic identity on your phone: a signing key and a peer address. That is how other members recognise you, and it never leaves your device except inside a backup you create yourself.

Records are signed

Every transaction, loan decision, meeting and roster change is signed with the recorder's key (Ed25519). Receiving phones check the signature and the sender's role before writing anything — being able to send a message is not the same as being allowed to write in the books.

Encrypted for your group

Group data is encrypted with a key derived from the group passphrase (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, XChaCha20-Poly1305). The passphrase is shared in person — never inside the invite link. Anything on the network is ciphertext.

Sync is peer-to-peer

Members' phones talk to each other directly over libp2p: an invite carries the inviter's address, every record carries its author's, and on each round a phone asks the members it knows what they hold and fetches whatever it missed. No vBank server in the middle — so no outage and no bill.

The phone's copy is encrypted too

The local database is encrypted with SQLCipher, keyed from a secret held in the device's hardware-backed keystore. A lost phone does not leak the books.

Conflicts resolve predictably

Each admin's records carry their own sequence numbers, so offline admins never collide. Group settings and the roster travel as signed snapshots ordered by version; the newest authorised change wins and everyone converges on the same books.

What this means for you. Keep your group passphrase and your backup PIN safe. Nobody — not us, not your co-members — can recover them for you, and without them a group's records cannot be decrypted on a new phone.
Getting started

Five steps to your first meeting

  1. Create your identity Open vBank, enter the name your group knows you by. No number, no email.
  2. Create a group or join one The treasurer creates the group and sets the rules and passphrase; everyone else scans an invite QR and types the passphrase they were told in person.
  3. Record the meeting As contributions come in, record them against each member. Schedule the next meeting before you leave.
  4. Lend and collect Members request loans; admins approve, pay out, and record repayments against the schedule.
  5. Back up Create a PIN-encrypted backup and keep it somewhere safe. It is the only way onto a new phone.

FAQ

Questions groups ask us

Does vBank hold or move our money?

No. Cash is handled by your group exactly as it is now. vBank records what was collected, lent and repaid; it has no payment feature and no wallet.

Do we need internet?

Not to use it. You need connectivity only when you want members' phones to exchange records, and even then it can wait — entries queue until a connection exists. A Wi-Fi hotspot at the meeting is enough.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Restore your backup file with its PIN on the new phone and you are back, including your group keys. Without a backup, your identity cannot be recreated — your group can still re-add you as a new member, but records you signed stay attributed to your old identity.

Can a member secretly change the books?

No. Members cannot write records at all, and anything an admin writes is signed and sequenced. A tampered or unsigned record is rejected by every other phone. Corrections happen through reversals, which stay visible in the history.

Who can see our records?

Only people who have your group's passphrase. What travels between phones is encrypted; without the passphrase it is meaningless.

Is there a fee?

No. There is no subscription, no transaction fee, no advertising, and no analytics.

Deleting your data

vBank stores your records on your own device only — there is no account to close and no copy of your data held by us. To delete everything, open Settings → Log out (removes your identity and keys from the phone) or uninstall the app. Encrypted records your group already exchanged remain on your co-members' phones, because those are their copies of the group's books; ask your group's admins to remove you from the roster if you want to stop receiving updates.
