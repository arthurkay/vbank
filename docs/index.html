What it is

Village banking (also called chilimba, merry-go-round, susu or ROSCA) is a group of people who meet regularly, each pay a fixed contribution into a common fund, and lend that fund to members at an agreed interest rate. The books are usually kept in a paper ledger held by one person — which is fine until the book is lost, disputed, or the treasurer misses a meeting.

vBank is that ledger, on the phones of every member at once. There is no company holding your money and no server holding your records: vBank moves no money. Cash is still handled in the room, exactly as it is today — vBank records what happened, proves who recorded it, and makes sure every member's copy agrees.

vBank is a record-keeping tool, not a bank. It is not a payment service, it holds no funds, and it is not regulated as a financial institution. Your group's money stays in your group's hands.

Features

Groups on your terms

Contribution amount and frequency, interest rate, late penalty, maximum loan multiple, minimum contributions before borrowing, and whether new members and loans need admin approval.

Contributions & penalties

Admins record contributions, penalties and withdrawals against a member. Every entry is signed and numbered, so two admins recording at once can never collide or overwrite each other.

Loans end to end

Request, approve or reject, disburse, then track a weekly repayment schedule with interest, late penalties, and automatic completion. Eligibility rules are checked before a request is made.

Meetings & attendance

Schedule meetings, mark who was present, excused or absent, tick off who paid, and close the meeting with the total collected.

Reports & statements

Group fund balance, contributions, loans out and repaid, penalties, and a per-member statement for any period — 1 month, 3 months, a year, or everything.

Corrections that leave a trail

Mistakes are reversed, not deleted: a member requests a reversal with a reason, an admin approves it, and both the original and the correction stay in the history.

Find anything

Search and filter every long list — activity, transactions, members, loans and meetings — by name, amount, date, type or status, with a live count of what matched.

Desktop, natively

Linux, macOS and Windows builds that follow each platform's own design system — Yaru on Ubuntu, macOS's sidebar and sheets, Fluent on Windows — over the same encrypted books.

Works offline

Everything works with no signal. Records queue on the phone and go out when you next have a connection or when members are together; the Sync screen shows exactly what is waiting.

Invites that expire

Add members with a QR code or link that works once and expires. The invite is signed by the admin who created it, so nobody can forge their way into your group.

Encrypted backups

Export your identity, groups and group keys as one PIN-encrypted file. Restore it on a new phone and carry on where you left off.

Reminders

Meeting reminders the day before, contribution-due nudges on meeting mornings, repayment reminders three days ahead, and overdue alerts. Each type can be switched off.

Roles that mean something

Owner, admin and member. Only owners and admins write records; members read their group and request loans or reversals. Rules are enforced again on every phone that receives a record.

Winding up cleanly

Transfer ownership when the treasurer changes, or dissolve the group: every member is paid out their net balance and the books become read-only.

How it works

No server, no account

There is no sign-up, no phone number and no email. When you first open vBank it generates a cryptographic identity on your phone: a signing key and a peer ID. That identity is what other members recognise you by, and it never leaves your device except inside a backup file you create yourself.

Records are signed

Every transaction, loan decision, meeting and roster change is signed with the recorder's key (Ed25519). When another member's phone receives it, the signature is checked and the sender's role is checked before anything is written. A record from someone who isn't an active admin of that group is discarded — being able to send a message is not the same as being allowed to write in the books.

Records are encrypted for your group

Group data is encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305 using a key derived from the group passphrase (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256). The passphrase is set by the person who creates the group and shared with new members in person — never inside the invite link. Anything vBank puts on the network is ciphertext; without your group's passphrase it is meaningless.

Sync is peer-to-peer

Phones exchange those encrypted records directly over IPFS — publishing to a per-group topic and finding each other through the network's distributed hash table. There is no vBank server in the middle, which also means there is no vBank outage, no subscription, and nobody to subpoena for your group's books.

The phone's copy is encrypted too

The local database is encrypted with SQLCipher, keyed from a secret held in the device's hardware-backed keystore. If your phone is lost, the books are not readable by pulling the file off it.

Conflicts resolve predictably

Each admin's records carry their own sequence numbers, so offline admins never produce colliding entries. Group settings and the member roster travel as signed snapshots ordered by version, so the newest authorised change wins and everyone converges on the same books.

What this means for you. Keep your group passphrase and your backup PIN safe. Nobody — not us, not your co-members — can recover them for you, and without them a group's records cannot be decrypted on a new phone.

Getting started

  1. Create your identity Open vBank, enter the name your group knows you by. No number, no email.
  2. Create a group or join one The treasurer creates the group and sets the rules and passphrase; everyone else scans an invite QR and types the passphrase they were told in person.
  3. Record the meeting As contributions come in, record them against each member. Schedule the next meeting before you leave.
  4. Lend and collect Members request loans; admins approve, disburse, and record repayments against the schedule.
  5. Back up Create a PIN-encrypted backup and keep it somewhere safe. It is the only way onto a new phone.

FAQ

Does vBank hold or move our money?

No. Cash is handled by your group exactly as it is now. vBank records what was collected, lent and repaid; it has no payment feature and no wallet.

Do we need internet?

Not to use it. You need connectivity only when you want members' phones to exchange records, and even then it can wait — entries queue until a connection exists.

What happens if I lose my phone?

Restore your backup file with its PIN on the new phone and you are back, including your group keys. Without a backup, your identity cannot be recreated — your group can still re-add you as a new member, but records you signed stay attributed to your old identity.

Can a member secretly change the books?

No. Members cannot write records at all, and anything an admin writes is signed and sequenced. A tampered or unsigned record is rejected by every other phone. Corrections happen through reversals, which stay visible in the history.

Who can see our records?

Only people who have your group's passphrase. What travels over the network is encrypted; other machines on the IPFS network may relay or cache those encrypted blocks without being able to read them.

Is there a fee?

No. There is no subscription, no transaction fee, no advertising, and no analytics.

Deleting your data

vBank stores your records on your own device only — there is no account to close and no copy of your data held by us. To delete everything, open Settings → Log out (removes your identity and keys from the phone) or uninstall the app. Encrypted records your group already exchanged remain on your co-members' phones, because those are their copies of the group's books; ask your group's admins to remove you from the roster if you want to stop receiving updates.

Note: encrypted blocks published to the IPFS network cannot be recalled once other nodes have cached them. They stay unreadable without your group's passphrase.
