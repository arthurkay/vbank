* An admin can suspend or reinstate ordinary members; only the owner can change admin roles or remove people.

Why members can't write records. In a savings group the books are kept by the people the group trusts with them. vBank mirrors that: the treasurer and any admins record what happened in the meeting, and every member gets a verified copy they can check. If a member spots an error they request a reversal, which an admin approves.

1. Your identity

  1. Open the app and tap “Get started”. Enter the name your group knows you by — this is what appears next to your entries.
  2. That's it. vBank creates a signing key and a peer ID on the phone. There is no password to remember at this stage, no phone number, no email, and nothing is sent anywhere.
  3. Make a backup early. Settings → Backup & Restore → “Create new backup”. See section 12 — this identity cannot be recreated from scratch.

2. Creating a group

Whoever keeps the books creates the group and becomes its owner.

  1. Groups → “New group”.
  2. Name it the way members will recognise it, e.g. “Ngombe Circle”.
  3. Set the contribution amount and how often it is due — weekly, biweekly or monthly.
  4. Choose a group passphrase. This encrypts the group's records. Pick something your members can repeat but strangers can't guess, and plan how you will tell it to new members in person. It is never included in an invite link, and it cannot be recovered or reset.
  5. Decide whether new members need approval. On: joining puts someone in a pending list until an admin approves them. Off: a valid invite admits them immediately.
  6. Create the group. Deriving the encryption key takes a moment on older phones — that's the passphrase being stretched deliberately.

You can adjust the rest of the rules any time in group → ⚙ Settings → Group configuration:

SettingWhat it does
Contribution & frequencyThe expected amount per member per cycle. Used for meeting totals and the “contribution due” reminder.
Loan interest (%)Flat interest added to a loan when it is approved. A 50.00 loan at 10% is repaid as 55.00.

Max loan (× contributions)Ceiling on what a member may borrow, as a multiple of what they have contributed.

3. Adding members

  1. Open the group → tap the “add person” icon. vBank publishes a fresh snapshot of the group and shows a QR code.
  2. Have the new member scan it from their phone (Groups → “Join another group” → Scan QR code), or send them the link with “Share invite link”.
  3. Tell them the group passphrase in person. Not over the same channel as the link — the link alone cannot decrypt anything.
  4. Each invite works once and expires after a few days. Generate a new one per member with “New invite”.
  5. If approval is on, approve them under ⚙ Settings → “Waiting for approval”.
Why two separate things? The QR gets the joiner to the right group and proves an admin invited them. The passphrase decrypts the books. Splitting them means a forwarded screenshot of a QR code is useless on its own.

4. Joining a group

  1. Groups → “Join another group”.
  2. Scan the QR the admin is showing you, or tap “Enter code manually” and paste the link.
  3. Type the group passphrase when prompted.
  4. Wait for the fetch. vBank finds the group's snapshot on the network, verifies the owner's signature and decrypts it. This needs a connection — do it while you have signal.
  5. If the group requires approval, you'll see “Waiting for approval” until an admin lets you in. Pull down to refresh later.

5. Recording contributions and penalties

  1. Open the group → “+ Transaction”.
  2. Pick the type. Contribution (money in from a member), Penalty (a fine, e.g. for lateness), Withdrawal (money out to a member that isn't a loan). Loans and repayments are not recorded here — they are recorded from the loan itself.
  3. Choose the member and enter the amount; the contribution amount is pre-filled.
  4. Add a note if it needs explaining later — “paid for August, was travelling”.
  5. Record it. The entry is signed, added to your books immediately, and queued to sync.

The group's Transactions tab lists everything newest first; Activity on the home screen shows the same across all your groups. Tap any entry for full details: who recorded it, when, its sequence number and its ID.

6. Loans

Requesting

  1. Group → Loans → “Request a loan”.
  2. Enter the amount; vBank checks eligibility as you type and tells you plainly if you are not eligible yet (too few contributions, over the multiple, or an open loan already).
  3. Choose the term in weeks and add a reason if the group expects one.
  4. Submit. If the group doesn't require approval and you're eligible, it is approved on the spot.

Approving and disbursing (admins)

  1. Open the loan from the Loans tab.
  2. Approve — you can approve less than requested — or Reject. An admin cannot approve their own request; another admin must.
  3. Hand over the cash, then tap “Record disbursement”. This writes the loan transaction and builds the weekly repayment schedule with interest included.

Repayments

  1. “Record repayment” on the loan. The amount is pre-filled with what the next installment needs, including any penalty.
  2. Payments apply to the oldest unpaid installment first. Partial payments are fine — the installment shows what is still outstanding.
  3. The loan closes itself when the total due is covered, and its status becomes completed.
  4. Late installments attract the group's late penalty, and a loan left unpaid 30 days past its last due date is flagged as defaulted.

7. Meetings and attendance

  1. Group → Meetings → “Schedule meeting”. Pick the date and time; members get a reminder the day before.
  2. On the day, open the meeting and set each member to Present, Excused or Absent, ticking “Paid” for those who contributed.
  3. Close it with “Complete meeting”. The total collected is pre-filled from who you ticked; correct it if the cash says otherwise, add notes, and complete.
  4. “Cancel meeting” if it doesn't happen — the record stays, marked cancelled.

Attendance is a record of the meeting; it does not itself create contribution entries. Record the money in the Transactions flow so each member's balance is right.

8. Reports and member statements

Group → 📊 icon. Choose 1 month, 3 months, 1 year or all time, and you get:

  • the group fund balance,
  • total contributions, loans disbursed, loans repaid and penalties,
  • the number of meetings and transactions in the period,
  • and a statement per member: contributed, loaned, repaid, and their outstanding balance.

This is the page to read out at an annual share-out, and the numbers every member can verify on their own phone.

9. Fixing mistakes

Nothing is deleted. To correct an entry:

  1. Open the transaction and tap “Request reversal”, giving the reason (“recorded twice”).
  2. An admin who didn't request it approves the reversal. The original is marked reversed, a compensating entry is written, and balances adjust.
  3. Both entries stay visible with who requested and who approved, so the trail is auditable at the next meeting.

10. Managing the roster

Group → ⚙ Settings → Members. From the ⋮ menu next to a person:

  • Make admin / Remove admin role — owner only.
  • Suspend / Reinstate — a suspended member stays in the books but can't transact.
  • Remove from group — owner only; you must give a reason, and if they have an outstanding loan you can choose to write it off (which marks the loan defaulted and records the amount).
  • Transfer ownership — see section 14.

11. Syncing between phones

vBank syncs peer-to-peer: your phone publishes encrypted records for the group and looks for the other members' phones over IPFS. There is no server, so “online” means “able to reach other members”, not “able to reach vBank”.

  • The indicator in the top-right of the home screen shows Online, Syncing, Connecting or Offline. Tap it for the Sync screen.
  • Sync status lists your node state, your peer ID, how many records are queued, syncing, synced or failed, and a live activity log.
  • Settings → Sync now forces a round.
  • A record that fails five times is marked failed and can be retried by hand from the Sync screen.
  • Members find each other directly: an invite link carries the inviter's network address, and every record a member publishes carries theirs, so once two phones have exchanged anything they can reach each other again without any directory or server.
  • Nothing is lost if a phone is asleep when a record is published: on every round each phone asks the members it knows for a list of what they hold and fetches whatever it is missing.
  • Pressing Back on the home screen sends vBank to the background rather than closing it, so it keeps syncing; it stops about 15 minutes later to save battery.

Tip for meetings: if the mobile network is poor, put the phones on the same Wi-Fi (a hotspot works) and tap Sync now — members' phones will find each other on the local network.

12. Backups and moving phones

  1. Settings → Backup & Restore → “Create new backup”.
  2. Choose a PIN of at least 6 characters. The file contains your identity, your signing key, your groups and their encryption keys — anyone with the file and the PIN can act as you, so treat both carefully.
  3. Export it to Drive, WhatsApp, a memory card, wherever you can find it again. Backups on the phone alone don't help if the phone is lost.
  4. On the new phone: “Restore from backup” on the welcome screen → import the file → enter the PIN. Your identity and groups come back, and you keep signing as the same person.
There is no recovery. No one can reset a forgotten backup PIN or a forgotten group passphrase, because no one else has a copy. This is the trade for having no server — write them down and keep them somewhere safe.

13. Reminders

Settings → Notifications. Each can be switched off independently:

  • Meeting reminders — 24 hours before a scheduled meeting.
  • Contribution due — on the morning of the meeting day.
  • Loan repayments — three days before an installment, and again when it is overdue.
  • Group activity — when new records, members or loan decisions arrive from other members.

Reminders are generated on your phone from your own records — nothing is scheduled through a server, so they work with no signal.

14. Ownership transfer and dissolution

Transferring ownership

Owner → ⚙ Settings → Members → ⋮ on an admin → “Transfer ownership”. You become an admin; the new owner's phone countersigns the change the next time it syncs, so the group has a signed record of the handover.

Dissolving the group

Owner → ⚙ Settings → “Dissolve group”. vBank refuses while any loan is still open or a reversal is pending, and tells you what is blocking it. Once it goes through, every member is paid out their net balance as a recorded distribution and the group becomes read-only — the books stay for reference.

15. Troubleshooting

Joining fails with “could not fetch the group”The inviting phone must have published the group recently and be reachable. Have the admin open the app (ideally on the same Wi-Fi) and try again.

Invite says used or expiredInvites work once. Ask for a new one from the invite screen.

My balance looks wrongOpen the Transactions tab and check for a duplicate or a missing entry, then use a reversal to correct it. Balances are derived from records, so fixing the record fixes the balance everywhere.
“Cannot dissolve: 1 loan(s) still outstanding”Close or write off the loans first.
Another member's phone shows different numbersBoth phones need to sync. After a sync, signed snapshots and per-author sequences reconcile the two copies automatically.

16. Good practice for groups

  • Two admins, not one. If the only admin loses their phone and has no backup, nobody can write records for the group any more.
  • Record in the meeting, not after. Entries are timestamped and signed; recording live keeps the books and the cash in step.
  • Read the report out loud at the end of a cycle. Every member can check the same numbers on their own phone — that transparency is the point.
  • Keep the passphrase spoken, never written in a group chat. Anyone who has it can read the group's records.
  • Make a backup after joining a new group, so the new group key is in it.
  • Agree the rules before you set them: interest, penalties and the loan ceiling are your group's decisions, and changing them mid-cycle causes arguments the app can't settle.

17. Finding things: search and filters

Once a group has run for a few months its lists get long. Every long list in vBank has the same two controls at the top: a search box and a row of filter chips. Search matches anywhere in the entry and ignores capitals, the chips narrow by kind or status, and the two combine — search inside the chip you have selected. When a list is narrowed you see a small “12 of 40” counter, and if nothing matches you get a “No matches” message naming what you searched for rather than an empty screen.

Search only appears once a list is long enough to need it, so a group with three transactions stays clean.

18. vBank on the desktop

The same app runs on Linux, macOS and Windows, with the same records, the same encryption and the same peer-to-peer sync. What changes is the look: each build follows its platform's own design language rather than imposing the phone's UI on your computer.

PlatformWhat it looks like
Linux Ubuntu's Yaru: a left pane of destinations (Groups, Activity, Meetings, Sync, Settings), tabbed group sections, settings grouped in sections of tiles, Ubuntu-style dialogs.
macOS A source-list sidebar window with a toolbar per page, tabbed group sections, sheets for forms and standard macOS alerts.
Windows Fluent: a navigation pane, page headers with a command bar, tabbed group sections, content dialogs, and info bars for feedback.

Two differences from the phone

  • Joining a group: Linux and Windows have no camera scanner, so paste the invite link (Groups → Join a group) instead of scanning a QR code. You still need the group passphrase.
  • Reminders: desktop notifications work on Linux and macOS. On Windows this build has no notification backend, so meeting and repayment reminders appear in the app rather than as system toasts.

Everything else — recording transactions, the full loan lifecycle, meetings and attendance, invites, reversals, backups, search and filters — is the same on desktop as on the phone, and the local database is encrypted on every platform.
